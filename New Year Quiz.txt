New Year Quiz 
1. What best describes Motherboard’s core innovation in interoperability? 
A. A faster trust-minimized cross-chain bridge 
B. A brand-new generalized Layer-1 blockchain 
C. A modular mental model commoditising communication and distribution 
D. A unified globally shared relayer infrastructure 
Answer: C 
2. Which actor in the Motherboard architecture is responsible for gas management across 
chains? 
A. Independent cryptographic verifier networks 
B. Decentralised transaction relayer networks 
C. Read-write blockchain RPC service providers 
D. Core routing smart contract modules 
Answer: B 
3. What does “non-discriminatory distribution” specifically enable? 
A. Exclusive chain partnerships and integrations 
B. Permissioned deployment across approved networks 
C. Fast cheap permissionless scaling to new chains 
D. Validator reward alignment and optimisation 
Answer: C 
4. When is a transaction officially considered valid on the destination chain? 
A. Once the message payload is delivered 
B. When cross-chain gas fees are settled 
C. After off-chain client execution completes 
D. When verifier report and relayer signature both match 
Answer: D 
5. How does Motherboard specifically expand Total Addressable Market (TAM) for 
applications? 
A. By reducing deployed smart contract complexity 
B. By enforcing protocol-wide standard interfaces 
C. By acting as a neutral distribution rail across chains 
D. By limiting deployment to approved ecosystems 
Answer: C 
6. Which definition best captures “messaging” as described in Andy Bohutsky’s article? 
A. Broadcasting transactions simultaneously to all chains 
B. Copying global blockchain state across networks 
C. Passing instructions or data so events trigger across chains 
D. Synchronizing block production times across ledgers 
Answer: C 
7. What problem did the “hub and spoke” interoperability model attempt to solve? 
A. Validator power concentration across networks 
B. Fragmentation of users and liquidity across chains 
C. Excessive gas fees on base layer networks 
D. Increasing smart contract development complexity 
Answer: B 
8. The earliest interoperability model (circa 2017) is compared to which telecommunication 
system? 
A. Early consumer internet broadband networks 
B. Modern cellular communication infrastructures 
C. Telegraph-style point-to-point communication 
D. Long-range satellite relay communication 
Answer: C 
9. What is the correct order of steps for a message to move from chain A to chain B? 
A. Deliver message, read event, validate proof, write state 
B. Read event, validate proof, deliver message, write state 
C. Validate proof, read event, write state, deliver message 
D. Read event, deliver message, validate proof, write state 
Answer: B 
10. Why are blockchains described as monolithic by design? 
A. They rely on fully centralized validator committees 
B. They prioritise execution speed over security 
C. They only support a single programming language 
D. They cannot natively communicate with external systems 
Answer: D 
11. According to Andy Bohutsky’s article, what is the first clear signal that interoperability is 
not solved? 
A. Persistently high transaction fees across chains 
B. Insufficient tooling available for developers 
C. Discriminatory distribution across blockchain ecosystems 
D. Slow and inconsistent transaction finality times 
Answer: C 
12. Why does the article argue that apps and asset issuers are “second-class citizens”? 
A. They lack the required technical infrastructure 
B. Their distribution is controlled by infrastructure providers 
C. They rely on chains to access liquidity 
D. They are unable to deploy across chains 
Answer: B 
13. Which statement best summarizes the current power dynamic in interoperability? 
A. Users dictate standards across all protocols 
B. Chains dictate how applications must behave 
C. Infrastructure dictates terms to value generators 
D. Value generators dictate infrastructure design 
Answer: C 
14. Which principle is emphasized as essential for the future of interoperability? 
A. Infrastructure ownership and value capture 
B. Permissioned access for verified participants 
C. Provider-centric standards and governance 
D. Neutral open trustless and permissionless systems 
Answer: D 
15. What percentage of the onchain world currently receives interoperability services from 
the largest interop protocol? 
A. Roughly half of existing blockchain networks 
B. About one quarter of deployed chains 
C. Approximately ten percent of the ecosystem 
D. Nearly one percent of all blockchains 
Answer: C

A. Highly restrictive validator admission requirements 
B. Carefully curated exclusive ecosystem partnerships 
C. Vendor-locking technical standards and interfaces 
D. Centralized governance-driven protocol decision making
